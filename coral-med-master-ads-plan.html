Why two campaigns, not one

Different searchers, different economics

"Primary care doctor near me" and "Botox Huntington Beach" are different buyer journeys with different keywords and different competitors — primary care competes with other family/internal medicine practices, while aesthetics competes with dedicated med spas like Reef Aesthetics and House of Aesthetics. Aesthetics keywords also tend to run at higher CPCs, but the services are cash-pay with meaningfully higher revenue per patient than a primary care visit, so a higher cost-per-click can still pencil out well on its own.

Mixing both into a single campaign would dilute Google's relevance signals on both sides and make it impossible to tell whether a dollar spent is producing a new primary care patient or a Botox booking. Two campaigns means two clean, independently reportable numbers: cost per new patient and cost per aesthetics inquiry.

Aesthetics-specific compliance

No before/after imagery, no outcome guarantees (no "guaranteed results" or specific measurements), and Xeomin referred to as physician-administered rather than self-administered — Google's Healthcare & Medicines policy scrutinizes cosmetic ads more heavily than standard local-service ads.
